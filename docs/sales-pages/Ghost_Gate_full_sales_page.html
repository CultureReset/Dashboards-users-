How it works

You ask. Ghost routes. Your systems do the work.

The goal is to hide the plumbing without hiding the control. Ghost turns ordinary requests into authorized actions and sends them to the right tool, device or service.

Voice & AI

Voice is the interface. Ghost is the gate.

The voice layer can be modular. A local speech model can keep audio inside the customer's environment where hardware allows. A hybrid setup can transcribe locally and send only permitted text to cloud intelligence. Cloud voice can also be used when the customer chooses it.

Private / Hybrid path

Microphone→Local speech-to-text→Ghost permissions→Local or cloud reasoning→Approved action
Important: encryption protects data in transit and at rest, but a service that actually processes audio must be able to access usable audio during that processing. Ghost's advantage is controlling what is sent, when, and to whom—not pretending encrypted ciphertext can be understood by ordinary speech models.
What is different

The closest products solve pieces. Ghost is designed around the control layer.

Smart home standards

Matter / MQTT

These help devices communicate and interoperate. Ghost can use technologies like these underneath the experience, but they do not themselves provide the full human-intent, permission, business-app, AI and execution layer.

Voice assistants

Siri / Alexa / Google-style assistants

They provide convenient interfaces inside particular ecosystems. Ghost's proposed distinction is vendor-neutral routing, customer-chosen storage and compute, modular connectors and a governed gate that can span multiple environments rather than making one vendor's ecosystem the center.

Automation / integration

Workflow tools and APIs

These connect software, but the user still usually deals with separate accounts, setup and interfaces. Ghost aims to package routing, identity, permissions, execution, device access and natural-language control into one managed experience.

The hard part is not inventing voice, APIs or smart-home protocols. The hard part is making all of those pieces reliable, governed, portable and simple enough that a family or business can use them without becoming an IT department.
